# Digital Identity Guidelines: NEXO Engineering & Contracting
Version 2.0 — bilingual (AR/EN), aligned to approved logo v1

## 1. Brand Essence

Name (EN): NEXO Engineering & Contracting
Name (AR): شركة نكسو للهندسة والمقاولات
Location: Baghdad, Iraq

Meaning: "Nexo" — the link, the connection, the nexus. The point where two
things are made to meet.

Positioning: Engineering and contracting delivered as one continuous line —
from design intent to built structure, with no handover gap.

Core metaphor: The Joint. Not a merger of two things into one, but two distinct
disciplines meeting at a precisely engineered connection. In structural terms:
a lap joint, a shear plane, a detail drawing at 1:5.

Tone: Technical, precise, authoritative, high-end industrial. Restrained.
Never decorative. Never boastful.

Tone in Arabic: Modern Standard Arabic (فصحى معاصرة). Professional register,
short sentences, no rhymed or ceremonial flourish. Technical terms may remain
in Latin script where that is standard practice in the Iraqi construction
sector (BIM, HSE, EPC, RC, MEP). Do not force-translate them.

## 2. Logo

### 2.1 The Mark
An abstract "X" built from two heavy diagonal members:

- Axis 1 (Design & Vision) — architectural precision, drafting, BIM.
- Axis 2 (Execution & Construction) — heavy civil contracting, structure.

The members cross without merging. At the centre they are separated by a fine
diagonal void, and two arms carry angled end-cuts. This void is THE JOINT — the
detail line where design meets execution. It is the single most identifiable
feature of the mark and must never be closed, filled, softened, or rounded.

Constructed from straight cuts only. No curves, no bevels, no gradients,
no metallic rendering, no outer glow, no drop shadow.

### 2.2 Lockups
- Master (LTR): symbol left, "NEXO" right, "ENGINEERING & CONTRACTING" set
  beneath NEXO and optically justified to its full width.
- Master (RTL): symbol RIGHT, Arabic wordmark left of it. Mirror the LAYOUT
  only. Never mirror the symbol itself.
- Stacked: symbol above, wordmark centred beneath. Use where horizontal
  space is under 4× the symbol width.
- Symbol only: app icons, favicons, embroidery, hard hats, watermarks,
  and any application under 24 mm / 80 px.

### 2.3 Clear space and minimum size
Clear space on all four sides = the stroke width of one arm of the X.
Nothing enters this zone — no text, no rules, no image edges.

Minimum size, full lockup: 32 mm print / 140 px screen.
Minimum size, symbol only: 8 mm print / 24 px screen.

### 2.4 Prohibited
Do not stretch or condense. Do not rotate. Do not recolour outside the
approved palette. Do not outline. Do not add effects. Do not close the
central void. Do not place the lockup on a busy photograph — use a solid
Deep Ink panel or a 60% Deep Ink scrim. Do not mirror the symbol in RTL.
Do not reconstruct the wordmark in a substitute typeface — use the
supplied vector master.

### 3.7 Contrast rules (non-negotiable)
- NEXO Gold on white is 2.4:1. It FAILS for text. On light surfaces gold is
  permitted only for the logo, rules, keylines, and shapes — never for
  body copy, labels, links, or any text below 24px.
- NEXO Gold on Deep Ink is 8.8:1. Approved for text at all sizes.
- Rule (#232833) is decorative. Any border a user must perceive to operate
  the interface uses Rule Strong (#545E70) or higher.
- Focus ring: 2px Gold Glow (#F3E5AB) with a 2px offset. Never removed.
- All interface text meets WCAG AA (4.5:1 normal, 3:1 large) in both scripts.

### 3.8 Gradients
Metallic gold rendering is permitted for 3D assets, foil stamping,
and signage renders only: #8A6F2E → #E4C77A → #C9A24E at 135°.
Never applied to the 2D logo. Never applied to type.

## 4. Typography System

Two scripts, one voice. Every tier below has a matched Latin and Arabic face.
All faces are open-licence (SIL OFL) and available on Google Fonts, so
web, print, and Office documents can all use the real thing.

### 4.1 Tier 1 — Display / Headings
Latin:   Archivo            weights 500, 600, 700, 800
Arabic:  Cairo              weights 500, 600, 700, 900

Cairo is a modern Kufi-influenced geometric face. Its flat terminals and
tight, structural counters are the closest Arabic analogue to Archivo's
slightly condensed geometry. Used for main titles and division headers.
Approved alternate: Alexandria.

### 4.2 Tier 2 — Body & Reading
Latin:   Plus Jakarta Sans  weights 400, 500, 600, 700
Arabic:  IBM Plex Sans Arabic  weights 400, 500, 600, 700

IBM Plex Sans Arabic is engineered for extended screen reading, has genuine
multi-weight Arabic (not synthesised bold), and shares a design lineage with
the mono tier below — giving the Arabic system the same technical accent the
Latin system gets from Plex Mono.
Approved alternate: Readex Pro.

### 4.3 Tier 3 — Utility & Technical Voice
Both scripts: IBM Plex Mono   weights 400, 500, 600

There is no credible monospaced Arabic. This is a deliberate system decision,
not a gap: division codes, specs, metadata, revision numbers, drawing
references, and dimensions remain in Latin script and Western numerals in
BOTH locales. Codes such as A—01, C—02, T—03 are treated as part numbers,
not language, and are never translated or transliterated.

### 4.4 Fallback stacks

Latin display:
    'Archivo', 'Segoe UI', system-ui, sans-serif
Arabic display:
    'Cairo', 'Noto Kufi Arabic', 'Segoe UI', Tahoma, sans-serif
Latin body:
    'Plus Jakarta Sans', system-ui, sans-serif
Arabic body:
    'IBM Plex Sans Arabic', 'Noto Sans Arabic', 'Segoe UI', Tahoma, sans-serif
Mono:
    'IBM Plex Mono', ui-monospace, 'Courier New', monospace

Fonts are self-hosted as WOFF2 and subsetted. Arabic subsets must retain the
full joining set plus Arabic-Indic numerals, even where unused, to prevent
tofu on user-generated content. Use font-display: swap.

### 4.5 Optical sizing across scripts
Arabic set at the same nominal size reads smaller and tighter than Latin.
Apply per-locale correction:

    Arabic display:  font-size × 1.08,  line-height × 1.10
    Arabic body:     font-size × 1.05,  line-height × 1.15

Minimum readable body size: 14px Latin / 15px Arabic.

### 4.6 Arabic typographic rules
- letter-spacing is ALWAYS 0 for Arabic. Tracking severs the joining
  strokes and produces broken, unprofessional-looking text. There are no
  exceptions, including headlines and logos.
- No text-transform. Arabic has no case. Never apply uppercase.
- No italic and no oblique. Emphasis in Arabic is carried by weight
  (600/700) or by NEXO Gold, never by slant.
- No synthetic bold. Use only the shipped weights.
- No kashida (tatweel) stretching for justification in digital interfaces.
- Do not justify Arabic body copy on the web. Set ragged-left.
- Harakat (diacritics) are off in interface and body copy. Permitted in
  ceremonial marketing lines and in the Arabic wordmark only.
- Arabic headlines take generous line-height (1.5 minimum) to clear
  descenders and dot clusters.

### 4.7 Numerals
Western Arabic numerals (0–9) are the default in BOTH locales for all
technical, financial, and dimensional content: quantities, dates, drawing
numbers, coordinates, BOQ figures, contract values.

Eastern Arabic-Indic numerals (٠–٩) are permitted only in ceremonial or
editorial Arabic marketing copy, never in tabular or technical data, and
never mixed with Western numerals in the same table or figure.

Use tabular-nums (font-variant-numeric) for all figures in tables.

### 4.8 Cross-script equivalence of typographic devices
The Latin system's signature devices do not translate. Use these equivalents
so both locales feel like the same brand:

Latin eyebrow:  IBM Plex Mono, uppercase, +0.12em tracking, Mute
Arabic eyebrow: IBM Plex Sans Arabic 600, 0 tracking, NEXO Gold,
                preceded by a 24px gold rule

Latin section head: Archivo 700 uppercase, +0.04em
Arabic section head: Cairo 700, 0 tracking, with a 2px gold underline rule

Latin small-caps label: Plex Mono uppercase, Mute
Arabic small label: Plex Sans Arabic 500, Mute, one step smaller

## 5. Bidirectional Layout (RTL)

- Build with CSS logical properties (margin-inline-start, padding-inline-end,
  border-inline-start, inset-inline). Never left/right.
- Set dir="rtl" lang="ar" at the document root for Arabic.
- Mirrored in RTL: page layout, navigation order, table column order,
  progress direction, breadcrumbs, arrows, chevrons, back/forward, indents,
  slide-in animations.
- NOT mirrored in RTL: the NEXO symbol, clock icons, checkmarks, media
  playback controls, external-link glyphs, photographs, logos of partners
  and clients, and all technical drawings.
- Technical codes and Latin-script strings inside Arabic paragraphs are
  wrapped in <bdi> or dir="ltr" to prevent bidi reordering. "A—01" must
  never render as "01—A".
- Mixed-script lines: never let the browser resolve direction implicitly.
  Declare it.
- Test every screen at 200% zoom in both directions before sign-off.

## 6. Visual Motifs & Textures

- The Drafting Grid: 1px repeating linear grid in Rule (#232833) over Deep
  Ink, with a radial mask fading toward the edges. Maximum 6% opacity.
  Never behind body copy.
- Structural Rules: 1px borders separating sections, blueprint-style.
  Rule (#232833) when decorative, Rule Strong (#545E70) when functional.
- The Joint: the central void of the logo, extracted as a standalone
  graphic device — a fine diagonal gold gap used as a section divider,
  a card corner detail, or a page-break mark.
- Monospace Badging: tracked-out uppercase Latin tags (A—01, C—02, T—03)
  identifying divisions and elements. Identical in both locales.
- Photography: real sites, real steel, real concrete. Cool, overcast or
  blue-hour light. Never stock imagery of handshakes, hard hats pointing
  at clipboards, or generic skylines. Never a gold colour-grade.
